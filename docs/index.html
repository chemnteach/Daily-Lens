Flesh-eating parasite resurfaces in Texas cattle
The U.S. Department of Agriculture has confirmed the first case of New World screwworm fly in American cattle in approximately six decades, detected in a calf in South Texas. The parasite, which feeds on living tissue, poses a significant threat to the nation's livestock industry after being successfully eradicated from the country decades earlier. This discovery marks an alarming development as officials work to contain the infestation and prevent its spread to other herds. The exact origins and implications for broader cattle populations remain under investigation by agricultural authorities.
