Soldier Charged With Using Classified Intel for Maduro Betting Profits
Master Sergeant Gannon Ken Van Dyke, a soldier stationed at Fort Bragg who participated in a January operation targeting Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, has been charged with using classified information to place winning bets on prediction market Polymarket, earning over $400,000. Federal prosecutors in New York brought the charges after investigating how Van Dyke accessed nonpublic operational details to inform his betting decisions. Military and political figures have reacted with criticism to the security breach, with a retired general expressing incredulity at the apparent lapse in judgment. The case has drawn responses from Trump administration officials, with some advocating for presidential clemency.

Federal Government Reclassifies Marijuana to Schedule III
The federal government has reclassified marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III, acknowledging potential medical benefits and reducing perceived harm potential. This administrative change places medical marijuana in the same category as certain pain medications, ketamine, and testosterone. The reclassification does not legalize marijuana nationally but facilitates expanded research into its medical applications and effects. Sources emphasize both the public health implications and potential business opportunities resulting from this policy shift, though the practical impact on state-licensed medical marijuana programs remains to be clarified.

Anthropic Withholds Advanced AI Model Over Security Concerns
Anthropic has announced Claude Mythos, an advanced AI model, but declined to release it publicly, citing cybersecurity risks. The company claims the model's capability to identify system vulnerabilities could enable large-scale cyberattacks if made widely available. This decision raises broader questions about the balance between technological advancement and public safety, private corporate control over powerful AI systems, and who should determine internet security standards. The incident highlights tensions between innovation, corporate responsibility, and governance in the rapidly evolving AI landscape.
