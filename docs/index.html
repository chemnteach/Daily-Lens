Virginia Supreme Court Strikes Down Voter-Approved Redistricting Plan
Virginia's Supreme Court has invalidated a voter-approved constitutional amendment that would have redefined the state's congressional redistricting process, striking down a measure that narrowly passed in April. The 4-3 decision represents a significant development in the ongoing national battle over partisan gerrymandering, as both parties pursue mid-decade redistricting efforts. Democrats viewed the plan as a means to counteract Republican-drawn maps, while the court's ruling eliminates those potential gains. The decision comes amid broader setbacks for Democrats in their broader redistricting strategy efforts across the country.

Alabama Republicans Pass Conditional Primary Plan
Alabama lawmakers approved legislation Friday that would direct the governor to schedule new primary elections if courts lift an injunction blocking a Republican-friendly congressional map. The measure is contingent on judicial approval to replace the current map with the GOP-preferred version. Demonstrators gathered outside the Statehouse to protest the plan, with critics arguing it undermines democratic representation. The move reflects an ongoing dispute over Alabama's congressional districts and represents Republicans' effort to proceed with electoral changes pending court decisions on the map's legality.
