Supreme Court Hears Arguments on Trump's Birthright Citizenship Order
The Supreme Court is hearing oral arguments on President Trump's executive order seeking to restrict birthright citizenship, a constitutional right granted under the 14th Amendment that currently applies to children born in the United States regardless of parental immigration status. Trump is attending the arguments, making history as the first sitting president to observe Supreme Court oral arguments. The case addresses whether the administration can limit birthright citizenship, a policy that could affect the legal status of hundreds of thousands of American-born children. The century-old understanding of birthright citizenship is now under constitutional scrutiny, with the Court's decision potentially reshaping immigration policy and citizenship law.

The mission represents continued progress in NASA's lunar exploration program and international space cooperation efforts.

Trump Signs Executive Order Restricting Mail-in Voting
President Trump signed an executive order on Tuesday that restricts mail-in voting and directs the creation of a national voter eligibility list. Democratic leaders, including House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries and Senate Majority Leader Schumer, have condemned the order as an "unlawful power grab." Critics argue the measure is unconstitutional and likely to face court challenges. Supporters claim the order aims to enhance election integrity, while opponents contend it represents an unprecedented federal overreach into election administration ahead of the midterms.

Supreme Court Strikes Down Colorado Conversion Therapy Ban
The Supreme Court ruled 8-1 that Colorado's law prohibiting conversion therapy for LGBTQ+ youth violates First Amendment protections for a Christian counselor challenging the restriction. The decision represents a significant legal victory for the therapist, who argued the ban infringed on her religious speech and counseling practices. The ruling potentially has broad implications for similar conversion therapy bans in other states. Supporters of the ban viewed it as necessary protection for minors, while the Court's majority sided with free speech considerations raised by the defendant.
