Supreme Court Eliminates Limits on Party Campaign Spending
The U.S. Supreme Court has ruled 6-3 that political parties can now spend unlimited amounts in coordination with their federal candidates, striking down longstanding Federal Election Commission restrictions on coordinated party expenditures. The decision, rooted in First Amendment grounds, removes one of the final barriers limiting the flow of money in federal elections. Left-leaning sources emphasize this as a significant weakening of campaign finance regulations, while right-leaning outlets frame it as a First Amendment victory. The ruling stems from a 2022 lawsuit challenging the FEC's enforcement of spending limits, and represents another major Supreme Court decision reshaping campaign finance law.

Topic 04 of 05
Supreme Court Upholds State Transgender Athlete Bans
The Supreme Court has ruled 6-3 that states can legally ban transgender girls and women from competing on school sports teams, upholding laws in Idaho and West Virginia. The conservative majority determined these bans do not violate the Constitution or Title IX, the federal law prohibiting sex discrimination in education. This decision affects similar legislation already passed in more than half the country. The ruling reflects the Court's recent pattern of deciding cases unfavorably to transgender rights claims. The case represents a significant legal moment for ongoing debates about transgender participation in athletics, balancing concerns about fairness in competition against transgender inclusion.
